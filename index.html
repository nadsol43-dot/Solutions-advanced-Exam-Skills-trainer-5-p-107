What can young people learn from older people?

Six steps from a blank page to a 220–260-word essay plan.

The task — Your class has had a discussion about what young people can learn from older people. You have made notes below:
  • Areas: money · relationships · work
  • Opinions heard: “You have to learn to control your finances when you live on your own.” · “Older people can tell who’s worth it.” · “They’ve been out in the working world, so they know what it’s like.”
  • Write an essay (220–260 words) discussing two of the areas, explaining in which area young people could learn most from older people, giving reasons in support of your answer.

Step 1 · Fire ideas first, judge later

Two minutes per area. Write short notes, not sentences — aim for 4–5 ideas in each area. Never delete an idea at this stage.

How to use: start the timer, type one idea and press Enter. Stuck? Tap Need a spark? — those are seeds to react to, not answers to copy.

Step 2 · Which area does each idea belong to?

Not every sentence is an idea. Marking the area forces you to notice why the idea matters.

How to use: choose Money, Relationships, Work — or Not an idea for a fact with no message. Instant feedback with a reason.

Step 3 · Dig for depth (the “three whys” ladder)

An essay idea is not “save money”. It is “save money → because rent and bills arrive before your salary → so you need a buffer, not a budget”. Climb the ladder three times.

How to use: pick one area, answer each step in a few words, and watch the sentence grow at the bottom. Copy that sentence straight into your plan.
